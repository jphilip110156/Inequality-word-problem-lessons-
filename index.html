Writing Linear Inequalities from Word Problems

Defining variables and translating "at least," "at most," and more into inequalities

Four Steps to Build an Inequality from a Word Problem

1
Define the variables. Find the two quantities being described, and let a variable stand for each one. By convention, let x represent the first quantity mentioned in the problem, and y represent the second. Always write the units — "number of tables," not just "tables."
2
Find the per-unit information. Look for a number attached to each variable — a cost per item, hours per unit, capacity per truck, and so on. That number becomes the coefficient in front of the variable. If nothing is mentioned, the coefficient is just 1.
3
Translate the keyword into a symbol. Phrases like "at least" or "at most" tell you which inequality symbol to use. See the reference table below.
4
Assemble the inequality. Combine the coefficients, variables, symbol, and the target number from the problem into one inequality.

Keyword-to-Symbol Reference Table

Phrase in the ProblemSymbolMeaning
"at least," "no less than," "a minimum of," "or more"≥greater than or equal to
"at most," "no more than," "a maximum of," "or fewer"≤less than or equal to
"more than," "exceeds," "greater than">strictly greater than
"less than," "fewer than," "under"<strictly less than
"exactly," "equal to," "is"=equal to
Tip: "at least" and "at most" both include the boundary number, which is why they use ≥ or ≤ (with the line underneath) rather than the strict > or <. A store that wants "at least 25 in stock" is perfectly happy with exactly 25.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Electronics Store

"An electronics store is ordering two types of Blu-ray players, Sony and LG. They want to have at least 25 Blu-ray players in stock."
Step 1 (define variables): The first quantity mentioned is Sony players, the second is LG players.
  Let x = number of Sony Blu-ray players.
  Let y = number of LG Blu-ray players.
Step 2 (per-unit info): None given — every player just counts as 1 toward the total, so both coefficients are 1.
Step 3 (keyword): "at least 25" → ≥ 25
Step 4 (assemble): total players in stock = x + y, and that must be ≥ 25.
x + y ≥ 25

Example 2 — Tables and Chairs

"A company builds tables and sets of chairs. Each table requires 2 hours of labor and each set of chairs requires 3 hours. Each week, the company can allow for at most 48 hours for labor."
Step 1 (define variables): The first quantity mentioned is tables, the second is sets of chairs.
  Let x = number of tables.
  Let y = number of sets of chairs.
Step 2 (per-unit info): Each table takes 2 hours → coefficient 2 on x. Each set of chairs takes 3 hours → coefficient 3 on y.
Step 3 (keyword): "at most 48 hours" → ≤ 48
Step 4 (assemble): total labor hours = 2x + 3y, and that must be ≤ 48.
2x + 3y ≤ 48

A bakery sells cupcakes and cookies. To cover daily costs, they need to sell at least 100 items per day.

Let x = number of cupcakes sold, y = number of cookies sold.
Keyword "at least" → ≥
x + y ≥ 100

Practice 2

A gym offers yoga classes (1 hour each) and spin classes (2 hours each). The instructor can teach at most 24 hours per week.

Let x = number of yoga classes, y = number of spin classes.
Keyword "at most" → ≤
x + 2y ≤ 24

Practice 3

A parking garage charges $5 for valet parking and $3 for self-parking. The garage wants to bring in more than $600 per day.

Let x = number of valet-parked cars, y = number of self-parked cars.
Keyword "more than" → >
5x + 3y > 600

Practice 4

Let x = number of adult tickets sold, y = number of child tickets sold.
